# A5 – Bracket Design

## Objective
The objective of this assignment is to design a bracket to the specifications detailed in the Appendix, in order to have the bracket hold a horizontal force of 500-800 lbf applied symmetrically by a the Uline strap provided in the resources section of the assignment. We were given three material options, and the option I chose was Aluminum 6061-T6. The calculation for the geometry of a beam would be done with regards to both stress and stiffness individually for each feature, and assumptions about the geometry were to be made from the information that was given. After determining the geometry in both regards, we were to make Multiview sketches that display the geometry that was calculated for in both regards, and reflect on the lessons learned throughout the process.

## Lessons Learned
For feature A, the minimum diameter of the cylinder, I noticed that my Stress calculations found it to be 0.523 in, while the Stiffness calculations found it to be 0.049 in. This was a giant difference, 0.474 in to be exact. Therefore, stress governed the minimum diameter by a whopping 0.474 in. 
To my knowledge, no error that I made snowballed all the way to the final results. I made many mistakes in the moment that I had to fix before it snowballed, or were transferred to my Multiview sketches. These included forgetting to square, cube, or fourth root my measurement to finish the calculation, more often than not leading me to believe I arrived at a super small measurement for a moment. Also, I occasionally would start to put the full applied force in an equation instead of half of it, and vice versa- putting half of the force when the full force is needed for the calculation. 
One assumption I made across many components/features throughout the assignment was the negligence of shear failure and deflection from shear. If this wasn't the case, many more calculations would have to have been included to ensure that shear stress does not require a larger minimum geometry. So as a result, this assignment would have been even more multifaceted, and more Multiview sketches could be made with respect to the geometry requirements that shear stress presents.
